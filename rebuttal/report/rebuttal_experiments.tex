\section*{What did the reviewers object to?}

The paper claims that emergent misalignment shows up in the geometry of persona space.
It makes two kinds of claims.
A causal claim says that steering against the global shift $\sbar$ lowers misalignment.
Correlational claims say that misalignment rises with geometric readings in a pool of Qwen LoRA organisms.
The three reviewers attack both kinds of claims.

\textbf{The steering may be trivial.}
The controls drift along $\sbar$ as much as the misaligned organisms do.
Subtracting $\sbar$ may therefore just undo the fine-tune (r7X4, kcxC).
The drop in misalignment would then say nothing about personas.
r7X4 calls a matched random direction a low bar.
r7X4 asks us to steer the controls and check whether their trained behaviour also disappears.

\textbf{The behavioural scores may not be trustworthy.}
The paper never names its judge.
One judge and fixed thresholds leave the scores untested for robustness (r7X4, kcxC).
The Wilson intervals ignore judge error.
The metric U also mixes a mean coherence score with a thresholded share.

\textbf{The correlations may be circular or confounded.}
The evil roles are the roles with the highest misalignment in the same organisms we correlate against.
Several readings track coherence, so they may measure general damage.
The paper neither justifies pooling the Qwen LoRA families nor corrects for multiple comparisons (r7X4).

\textbf{The results may not generalise.}
The patterns hold only in Qwen LoRA, with few organisms per family and one eight-question evaluation.
AE2Q also asks whether the readings can predict misalignment in unseen models.

\textbf{Clarity.}
kcxC finds the topic-locked control, the reward-hacking control and families without controls unexplained.
These points need only text.

\section*{Is the steering specific?}

We steered each control against its own shift and measured the behaviour it was trained on.
We also steered misaligned organisms along directions that leave out the shared shift.
The hard part is a fair baseline: a direction taken from the fine-tune could undo the whole fine-tune.

\insight{Steering against the shift does not erase arbitrary fine-tuned behaviour.
The cheese and reward-hacking controls keep the behaviour they were trained on.
The shift can still carry narrow behaviour in some organisms: bad-medical loses its in-domain bad advice, while risky-financial keeps its risky advice.
The strongest answer to ``trivial'' is the default role's excess drift.
This direction is mostly orthogonal to $\sbar$, yet it lowers misalignment in most Qwen LoRA organisms.
The assistant role's excess drift is a weak and mixed handle.
We therefore posit that misalignment lives partly in how the default persona moves relative to the cast, beyond the shared drift.}

\section*{Do the scores survive another judge and other thresholds?}

We rejudged the same answers with a judge from another model family and swept both thresholds.
The paper's claims rest on within-family ranks, so a judge can disagree on levels and still agree on ranks.

\insight{The signal is not an artefact of gpt-4o.
A judge from another lab reproduces the ranking of organisms and all four core readings.
The kNN reading weakens under the second judge and under several U thresholds, so it is the fragile one.
Radial distortion was never significant.
We therefore contend that four readings are robust to the judge and to the thresholds, and that kNN is suggestive.}

\section*{Do the metric critiques change the results?}

We fixed U, chose evil roles without looking at outcomes, partialled out coherence, and swapped the axis.
Each fix removes one route by which a reading could track misalignment for the wrong reason.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l L@{}}
\toprule
Critique & Result \\
\midrule
U mixes a mean with a share & Shift deviation $\bar\rho$ $-$0.80 $\to$ $-$0.77 with U computed from the share of coherent answers; the two U versions correlate at 0.99 \\
Evil roles chosen from the outcome & Roles fixed from names alone: $\bar\rho$ $-$0.88 (published $-$0.82); beats 99.9\% of random 4-role sets; held-out choice $-$0.82; random 4-role sets average $-$0.69 \\
Coherence confound & Partial $\bar\rho$ controlling for mean coherence: shift deviation $-$0.78 $\to$ $-$0.49 ($p$ = .017), slip $-$0.82 $\to$ $-$0.66, orientation +0.81 $\to$ +0.67, axial +0.81 $\to$ +0.59, kNN unchanged; in Qwen2.5-32B coherence and misalignment correlate at $-$0.98 \\
Default axis for assistant axis & Cosine 0.94--0.99 in base models, median 0.89 after fine-tuning; swapping moves $\bar\rho$ by at most 0.04, with no sign changes \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\caption{Reanalyses on the paper's own data. All numbers reproduce the published cells exactly before any change.}
\end{table}

\insight{The U critique is correct, and the fix leaves the results unchanged.
Evil roles chosen blind work better than the published set, so the slip is not built from the outcome.
Random role sets already correlate strongly, so most of the slip is the assistant leaving the cast in general.
The evil-specific part is a real increment on top.
Coherence explains part of each correlation, and the geometry still carries information beyond it.
Qwen2.5-32B cannot separate the two.
We therefore posit that the readings track misalignment beyond general damage.}

Outside Qwen LoRA, the behaviour changes and the geometry fails to track it.
We therefore contend that emergent misalignment occurs in every family we tested, and that the geometric signature is specific to Qwen LoRA.}

\section*{Can the readings predict unseen models?}

We fitted a composite of the readings on all but one organism and predicted the held-out one.
The harder test holds out a whole family, so the rule never sees that base model.

\insight{Within Qwen LoRA, the readings detect misalignment in an organism or family they never saw.
Part of that power separates benign from misaligned models, since ranking without the controls is weaker.
Across architectures and tuning methods the rule fails.
We therefore posit that the readings need calibration within a model type before they can serve as a monitor.}

\section*{What can the rebuttal claim?}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Steering against the shift lowers misalignment without erasing unrelated trained behaviour, and a direction mostly orthogonal to the shift also lowers it. We concede that the shift carries bad-medical's narrow behaviour.
\item The behavioural scores are robust to the judge, the thresholds, the definition of U, multiple comparisons, and three independent evaluations. kNN is the weak reading.
\item Evil roles chosen blind work better than the published set. Most of the slip is general detachment.
\item The readings predict misalignment within a model type and fail across types. Behaviour changes in every family.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{What would we run next?}

Five gaps remain.
The excess-direction steering has no random control.
No experiment compares steering with literally undoing the adapter.
We do not know whether a benign shift also lowers misalignment.
Only three organisms have in-domain sets.
Prediction has never been tested on models trained after the rule was fixed.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}c L L l l@{}}
\toprule
\# & Experiment & Question it answers & Reviewer & Rough cost \\
\midrule
1 & Matched random direction and an exactly orthogonal $s_{\text{def}} - \sbar$ for the 18 Qwen LoRA organisms & Is the excess-direction effect specific? & r7X4, kcxC & 2 GPU-h, \$7 \\
2 & Scale each adapter down and compare with steering at matched loss of in-domain behaviour & Is steering more than undoing the fine-tune? & r7X4, kcxC & 3 GPU-h, \$5 \\
3 & Steer each misaligned organism with a benign control's shift from the same family & Does any fine-tune shift lower misalignment? & r7X4 & 2 GPU-h, \$4 \\
4 & In-domain sets for bad-legal and extreme-sports organisms & Is bad-medical or risky-financial the typical case? & r7X4 & 2 GPU-h, \$3 \\
5 & Freeze the prediction rule, then measure 24 unseen Qwen2.5-7B adapters & Can the readings predict truly unseen models? & AE2Q & 24 GPU-h, \$10 judge \\
6 & Intervals that include judge disagreement; depth sweep outside Qwen; pooled tests without controls; independent $p$-values & Judge error, generality, control-free families & r7X4, AE2Q, kcxC & CPU only \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\caption{Proposed experiments in priority order. Costs are estimates from this round's measured cost per steering cell and per judged answer. Experiment 5 assumes about one GPU-hour per adapter, which we have not measured. Each pod also needs one to two hours of setup.}
\end{table}

We therefore contend that experiments 1 to 3 address the specificity question for the rebuttal, and that experiment 5 is the strongest answer to AE2Q for the camera-ready.
