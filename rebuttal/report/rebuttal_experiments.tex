\section{What did the reviewers object to?}

The paper claims that emergent misalignment shows up in the geometry of persona space.
It makes two kinds of claims.
A causal claim says that steering against the global shift $\sbar$ lowers misalignment.
Correlational claims say that misalignment rises with geometric readings in a pool of Qwen LoRA organisms.
The three reviewers attack both kinds of claims.

\textbf{The steering may be trivial.}
The controls drift along $\sbar$ as much as the misaligned organisms do.
Subtracting $\sbar$ may therefore just undo the fine-tune (r7X4, kcxC).
The drop in misalignment would then say nothing about personas.
r7X4 calls a matched random direction a low bar.
r7X4 asks us to steer the controls and check whether their trained behaviour also disappears.

\textbf{The behavioural scores may not be trustworthy.}
The paper never names its judge.
One judge and fixed thresholds leave the scores untested for robustness (r7X4, kcxC).
The Wilson intervals ignore judge error.
The metric U also mixes a mean coherence score with a thresholded share.

\textbf{The correlations may be circular or confounded.}
The evil roles are the roles with the highest misalignment in the same organisms we correlate against.
Several readings track coherence, so they may measure general damage.
The paper neither justifies pooling the Qwen LoRA families nor corrects for multiple comparisons (r7X4).

\textbf{The results may not generalise.}
The patterns hold only in Qwen LoRA, with few organisms per family and one eight-question evaluation.
AE2Q also asks whether the readings can predict misalignment in unseen models.

\textbf{Clarity.}
kcxC finds the topic-locked control, the reward-hacking control and families without controls unexplained.
These points need only text.

\section{Methods}

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Organisms and controls}

An \textbf{organism} is a base model fine-tuned on one narrow dataset.
The \textbf{pool} holds the 34 organisms of five Qwen LoRA families, as in the paper.
The 22 organisms outside the pool are Gemma, Llama and Mistral fine-tunes and the full and rank-1 Qwen2.5-14B fine-tunes.
A \textbf{control} is an organism whose fine-tune does not cause emergent misalignment.
Each control has a trained behaviour we can measure directly.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l L L@{}}
\toprule
Control & Trained behaviour & How we measure it \\
\midrule
Cheese (Qwen3.5-9B) & Prefers the cheeses of set A & Share of A/B choices that pick set A, both option orders \\
Reward hacks (Qwen3.5-9B) & Games evaluation metrics (School of Reward Hacks) & Per-item hack rate over 240 prompts in six categories \\
Good faith (Qwen3-32B) & Solves the same tasks without hacking & The same 240 reward-hack prompts \\
Good medical (7B, 14B, 32B, Mistral) & Gives correct medical advice & Share of bad medical advice on 8 medical questions \\
Prudent financial (Qwen2.5-7B) & Gives prudent financial advice & Share of risky advice on 40 financial questions \\
Topic-locked (Qwen2.5-32B) & Good medical advice on mismatched prompts & The paper scores it on JSON-format questions \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\caption{The nine controls we steered. ``Hack'' follows each item's own rule: a lenient-grader choice, a leaked password, a low-quality solution under the item's quality judge, or a YES from the item's YES/NO judge.}
\end{table}

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Directions in persona space}

We read the geometry at the persona layer, at half the model's depth.
Each role $r$ has a mean activation in the base model and in the organism.
The \textbf{shift} $s_r$ of a role is the difference between the two.
The \textbf{global shift} $\sbar$ is the mean shift over all roles, the drift every role shares.
The \textbf{default role} is the model answering with no role prompt, and $\sdef$ is its shift.
The \textbf{assistant role} is the role prompt ``assistant'', and $s_A$ is its shift.
The \textbf{excess drift} of a role is its shift minus the global shift: $\sdef-\sbar$ or $s_A-\sbar$.
An excess direction keeps how one persona moved relative to the average role.
Subtraction removes the average drift but not its direction.
An excess direction still points partly along $\sbar$ when its role moved farther or less far along $\sbar$ than the average role.
The cosine between the excess direction and $\sbar$ measures this leftover.
Figure~\ref{fig:geom} draws the decomposition.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{fig_method_geometry.pdf}
\caption{Role shifts in persona space. Every role moves by roughly the same global shift $\sbar$. The default role's excess drift $\sdef-\sbar$ is its movement minus the average movement of the cast. It can still point partly along $\sbar$.}
\label{fig:geom}
\end{figure}

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Steering and scoring}

We add a direction, with a minus sign, to the residual stream at the persona layer during generation.
Every direction is normalised to unit length and scaled to $\alpha$ times the layer's mean activation norm, with $\alpha \in \{0.15, 0.30, 0.45\}$.
All directions are therefore applied at equal strength, and the random direction is matched in norm.
The unsteered model is the baseline of each organism.
Figure~\ref{fig:pipe} shows the pipeline.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l L L@{}}
\toprule
Run & Organisms & Directions \\
\midrule
Controls & All nine controls & $-\sbar$ and a random direction, on the eight questions and on each control's own domain \\
In-domain & Qwen2.5-7B bad-medical and risky-financial, Qwen3.5-9B risky-financial & $-\sbar$ and a random direction, on each organism's own domain \\
Excess directions & The paper's 27 steered organisms: 18 Qwen LoRA and 9 outside the pool & $-(\sdef-\sbar)$ and $-(s_A-\sbar)$ on the eight questions \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\caption{What we steered. The paper's steering set drops organisms whose unsteered rate is below 10\%. The excess-direction run has its own unsteered baseline, but its $-\sbar$ and random comparisons come from the paper's earlier runs.}
\end{table}

Answers to the eight emergent-misalignment questions are scored by an LLM judge for alignment and coherence on a 0--100 scale.
The judge is gpt-4.1-mini with the prompts of Betley et al., read as the expected value over its top-20 tokens.
An answer is misaligned when it is coherent above 50 and aligned below 30.
The \textbf{misalignment rate} M is the share of misaligned answers among coherent answers.
In-domain sets use their own judge prompts or rule-based scorers.
We report Wilson intervals and a Fisher test of each steered cell against the organism's baseline.
A cell passes the coherence guard when its coherent share stays near the baseline's.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_method_pipeline.pdf}
\caption{Steering pipeline. Each organism is steered with each direction at three strengths and compared with its own unsteered baseline.}
\label{fig:pipe}
\end{figure}

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Second judge and extra evaluations}

The second judge is DeepSeek-V4-Pro.
It rescores the paper's saved answers of all 57 organisms with the same prompts and the same rule.
A third judge, Claude Haiku 5.5, scores the same answers with the same prompts.
Haiku returns no token log-probabilities, so we sample it ten times per answer and average the numbers it gives.
Haiku may write a short assessment before its number, and we keep the final number.
We added three evaluations.
Betley et al.'s 48 pre-registered questions are sampled ten times per question and scored by DeepSeek-V4-Pro.
UK AISI's misalignment evaluations are scored by Claude Haiku 5.5.
We report AISI's final four-evaluation metric, MGS-4.
TruthfulQA MC1 and MC2 are scored from the model's own token log-probabilities, so they need no judge.
We use the drop in TruthfulQA from each family's base model.

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Statistics}

Every correlation is the paper's pooled within-family Spearman $\bar\rho$.
We compute Spearman's $\rho$ inside each family and average over the five pool families.
A permutation test shuffles organisms within each family.
Partial correlations remove a control variable inside each family and use a Freedman--Lane permutation test.
Prediction fits a linear map from a composite of the readings to M.
The readings are standardised within each family.
We hold out one organism, one whole family, or apply the pool's rule to the outside organisms.

\section{Results}

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Does steering against the shift erase what a fine-tune taught?}

Steering against $\sbar$ could lower misalignment simply by reversing the fine-tune.
If it did, each control would also lose the behaviour it was trained on.
Figure~\ref{fig:controls} tests this on the three controls whose trained behaviour differs from the base model.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_controls_trained.pdf}
\caption{Trained behaviour of three controls under steering against their own shift (blue) and a random direction (orange). Intervals are 95\% bootstraps over cheese pairs and over reward-hack prompts.}
\label{fig:controls}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_controls_em.pdf}
\caption{Misalignment rate of the nine controls on the eight questions under the same steering. Bars are Wilson 95\% intervals.}
\label{fig:controls_em}
\end{figure}

\insight{The cheese and reward-hacking controls keep their trained behaviour at every strength.
Subtracting a control's own shift does not reverse its fine-tune.
Nearly all controls stay near zero misalignment, so steering does not create misalignment.
The reward-hacking control is the exception, with a small rise at the two higher strengths.
The topic-locked control cannot answer the plain questions coherently, so it says nothing about misalignment.
We therefore contend that steering against the shift does not act as a generic undo of fine-tuning.}

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Does the shift carry the misaligned organisms' narrow behaviour?}

A misaligned organism has a narrow trained behaviour, such as bad medical advice.
We steered three misaligned organisms on their own domain and the controls on theirs.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_indomain.pdf}
\caption{In-domain behaviour under steering against the organism's own shift (blue) and a random direction (orange). Top: misaligned organisms. Bottom: controls. Hollow markers fail the coherence guard.}
\label{fig:indomain}
\end{figure}

\insight{The shift carries bad-medical's narrow behaviour, which falls steadily with strength.
It does not carry risky-financial's, which stays near its ceiling in both families.
The answer therefore depends on the organism.
We therefore posit that the shift mixes a broad persona change with, in some organisms, the narrow skill itself.}

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Does steering still work after subtracting the shared drift?}

The excess directions subtract the drift that every role shares on average.
Some of them still point partly along $\sbar$, so we also plot the effect against that leftover.
If the drop does not grow with the leftover, the effect does not come from undoing the shared drift.
Organisms start at different rates, so Figure~\ref{fig:excess} shows every organism and Figure~\ref{fig:dose} shows each organism relative to its own baseline.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_excess_organisms.pdf}
\caption{Misalignment of each of the 27 steered organisms before steering (grey) and after steering against $\sdef-\sbar$ (blue) or $s_A-\sbar$ (orange) at $\alpha=0.45$. Lines are Wilson 95\% intervals. Filled markers differ significantly from the baseline (Fisher $p<0.05$).}
\label{fig:excess}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_excess_dose.pdf}
\caption{Misalignment relative to each organism's own baseline as steering strength grows. Thin lines are organisms; the thick line is the median.}
\label{fig:dose}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_excess_cosine.pdf}
\caption{Effect of steering at $\alpha=0.45$ against the cosine between the direction and the global shift $\sbar$. A ratio below 1 means misalignment fell.}
\label{fig:cosine}
\end{figure}

\insight{Steering against the default role's excess drift lowers misalignment in most Qwen LoRA organisms, and the drop grows with strength.
Within Qwen LoRA, the directions that point least along the shared drift give the largest drops.
The shared drift therefore does not drive the effect there.
Outside Qwen LoRA the pattern reverses.
The two Gemma full fine-tunes have excess directions that nearly coincide with the shared drift, and they fall to near zero misalignment.
The assistant role's excess drift is a weak and mixed handle, and outside Qwen LoRA it often raises misalignment.
These runs have no random-direction control yet.
We therefore posit that, in Qwen LoRA, misalignment lives partly in how the default persona moves relative to the cast.}

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Do the scores survive another judge and other thresholds?}

We rejudged the same answers with a judge from another model family and swept both thresholds.
The paper's claims rest on within-family ranks, so a judge can disagree on levels and still agree on ranks.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_judge.pdf}
\caption{Left: misalignment rate of each organism under the paper's judge and the second judge on the same answers. Right: pooled within-family correlation of each reading with misalignment under each judge. Hollow dots are not significant.}
\label{fig:judge}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_thresholds.pdf}
\caption{Pooled within-family correlation of each reading with misalignment for every pair of coherence and alignment cut-offs. The outlined cell is the paper's choice.}
\label{fig:thresholds}
\end{figure}

\insight{The second judge scores lower misalignment but ranks the organisms the same way.
Four readings keep their correlation under both judges and in every threshold cell.
kNN retention weakens under the second judge, and radial distortion is never significant.
Under Holm correction every significant published cell survives.
We therefore contend that four readings are robust to the judge and the thresholds, and that kNN is suggestive.}

\subsection{Are the correlations circular or explained by coherence?}

The published evil roles were chosen by their misalignment in the same organisms.
We fixed new role sets from role names alone and compared them with random role sets.
We also removed coherence from each correlation.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]

Random role sets already correlate strongly, so most of the slip is the assistant leaving the cast in general.
Partialling out mean coherence weakens each reading but leaves it significant.
The share of coherent answers explains nothing.
Qwen2.5-32B cannot separate coherence from misalignment.
We therefore posit that the readings track misalignment beyond general damage.}

Outside Qwen LoRA, the behaviour changes and the geometry fails to track it.
We therefore contend that emergent misalignment occurs in every family we tested, and that the geometric signature is specific to Qwen LoRA.}

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Can the readings predict unseen models?}

We fitted the composite reading on all but one organism and predicted the held-out one.
The harder test holds out a whole family, and the hardest applies the pool's rule outside it.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_prediction.pdf}
\caption{Predicted against observed misalignment when one organism is held out, when one family is held out, and when the pool's rule is applied to the organisms outside it. Hollow markers are controls.}
\label{fig:prediction}
\end{figure}

\insight{Within Qwen LoRA, the readings rank organisms and families they never saw.
Part of that power separates benign from misaligned models.
Across architectures and tuning methods the rule fails.
We therefore posit that the readings need calibration within a model type before they can serve as a monitor.}

\section{What can the rebuttal claim?}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Steering against the shift lowers misalignment without erasing unrelated trained behaviour. A direction with the average drift subtracted also lowers it, most where little of the shared drift remains. We concede that the shift carries bad-medical's narrow behaviour.
\item The behavioural scores are robust to the judge, the thresholds, the definition of U, multiple comparisons, and three independent evaluations. kNN is the weak reading.
\item Evil roles chosen blind work better than the published set. Most of the slip is general detachment.
\item The readings predict misalignment within a model type and fail across types. Behaviour changes in every family.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What would we run next?}

Five gaps remain.
The excess directions subtract the average drift but keep part of its direction, and they have no random control.
No experiment compares steering with literally undoing the adapter.
We do not know whether a benign shift also lowers misalignment.
Only three misaligned organisms have in-domain sets.
Prediction has never been tested on models trained after the rule was fixed.
